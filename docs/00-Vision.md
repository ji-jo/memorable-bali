# 00 — Vision

## The one sentence

**The most enjoyable way to discover Bali.**

## The feeling we are selling

> "Someone already did the research for me."

That is the entire product. A visitor opens the app and the anxiety of planning drops away, because the hard part — sorting the extraordinary from the merely popular — has already been done by someone with taste.

## The problem

Bali is over-documented and under-curated. Google Maps knows about ten thousand places and ranks them by review volume. TripAdvisor knows which ones have the most photographs. Neither knows which are *worth your Tuesday*.

So planning a trip means opening fifteen tabs, reading blog posts written for SEO rather than for you, and eventually picking the thing that appeared most often. The result is a trip assembled from consensus rather than judgement — everyone visits the same six places, at the same time of day, and comes home with the same photographs.

## The position

**Curated, not comprehensive.** Roughly fifty places for the whole island. Adding the fifty-first should require deleting one.

The dataset is small on purpose. Fifty places is a number a person can hold in their head, browse in an evening, and trust. Ten thousand is a search problem; fifty is a recommendation. The moment we start optimising for coverage we become a worse Google Maps, and Google Maps is very good at being Google Maps.

Everything follows from this:

- Every place carries **at least two specific, useful tips** — what time to arrive, what will go wrong, what most people miss. Generic advice fails the standard.
- We say when somewhere is crowded, overpriced, or dangerous. Tegallalang is "a queue for a swing" by eleven. Kelingking injures people every season. Angel's Billabong has killed swimmers. Honesty is not a tonal choice here; it is the only thing that makes the recommendations worth anything.
- No sponsored placements. Ever. The instant a listing can be bought, the curation is worthless and so is the app.

## What we are not

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| Google Maps | They win on coverage, routing, and live data. We are not competing there — we use their APIs. |
| TripAdvisor | Review aggregation is a popularity contest. We are making editorial judgements and signing our name to them. |
| A booking platform | No payments, no inventory, no commission. Ferry and tour links go out to the operator. |
| A social network | No user reviews, no photo feeds, no follower counts in the MVP. Every social feature dilutes the curation. |
| A comprehensive directory | Fifty places. If it grows past ~80, the product has failed at its one job. |

## Who it is for

A visitor with somewhere between one day and two weeks in Bali who wants a *good* trip rather than an exhaustive one. They have limited time, they do not want to spend it researching, and they would rather see six remarkable places properly than twenty adequate ones in a rush.

They are not a backpacker optimising for cost, and not a package tourist who wants everything arranged. They want taste, applied on their behalf.

## How we will know it worked

- Someone finishes onboarding and immediately adds three places to an itinerary — the recommendations landed.
- Someone reads a tip, changes their plan because of it, and the day goes better.
- Someone recommends the app by saying what is *in* it, not what it *does*.

## Principles

1. **Quality over quantity.** Always. This is the tiebreaker for every product argument.
2. **Show, don't list.** Photography first, text second. This is a visual product about a beautiful place.
3. **Honest over enthusiastic.** The crowded truth beats the marketing copy.
4. **Fast and quiet.** No modals begging for signup, no notification prompts, no cookie theatre.
5. **Works offline-ish.** Local data, local itineraries. A patchy connection in a rice field should not break the app.

## The bet

That in a category defined by having *more* — more listings, more reviews, more filters — the differentiated product is the one that has *less*, chosen well.
